# Worked examples

## "No vehicles in the park"

A classic teaching problem. Rule: "No vehicles may enter the park." Does it
bar (a) a decommissioned tank mounted as a war memorial, (b) a child's
battery-powered toy car, (c) a motorised wheelchair?

- **Language.** "Vehicle" plainly covers cars and lorries; its edges are
  vague. A tank is a vehicle by kind but is not being driven; a toy car sits
  at the edge; a wheelchair is a mobility aid that moves on wheels. Language
  marks the zone of doubt without resolving it.
- **System.** If the legal order protects the mobility of persons with
  disabilities, a reading that bars wheelchairs conflicts with higher law.
- **History.** Suppose the council minutes show the rule followed
  complaints about motorbikes on footpaths: the occasion was traffic.
- **Purpose.** Safety and quiet. A static tank threatens neither; a toy car
  barely; a wheelchair user is the kind of visitor the park serves.
- **Result.** None is barred. The tank and the toy fall outside the
  purpose at the vague edge of the word (vagueness is resolved by purpose);
  the wheelchair is excluded by purpose and independently by conformity
  with higher law. Confidence: strong; no case law assumed.

The lesson: the words set the range; system and purpose decide within it.

## Effectiveness overriding the most natural reading (EU law)

The Treaty provision on equal pay (then Art. 119 EEC, now Art. 157 TFEU)
said that each Member State "shall ensure" the principle of equal pay for
men and women is applied. Could a worker rely on it against a private
employer?

- **Language.** "Each Member State shall ensure" reads as an obligation on
  states, not a rule binding employers.
- **Purpose.** The provision served both an economic aim (preventing
  competitive distortion from cheap female labour) and a social one
  (equality). Both would be defeated if it bound only states.
- **System.** Effectiveness (*effet utile*): Treaty provisions are read to
  have practical effect.
- **Result.** In *Defrenne v Sabena* (Case 43/75) the Court of Justice held
  the provision directly effective, including between private parties.
  Purpose and system prevailed over the most natural reading of the words,
  without going beyond what they could bear: "ensure" does not say that
  only states are bound.

When citing a decision like this in an answer, retrieve and read it first.
