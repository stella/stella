# Branch rules

Some branches of law change the weight of the methods.

- **Criminal law.** No crime and no penalty without a prior, clear law
  (*nullum crimen, nulla poena sine lege*). Read offences and penalties
  strictly; never extend them by analogy or teleological reduction to the
  accused's detriment. Analogy in the accused's favour (defences, mitigating
  rules) is generally allowed. In common-law systems, a genuine residual
  ambiguity is resolved for the accused (rule of lenity); continental
  systems reach a similar result through strict construction.
- **Administrative sanctions.** Most systems extend the criminal-law
  guarantees, at least in part, to administrative penalties; the European
  Convention on Human Rights does so for penalties that are criminal in
  substance.
- **Tax law.** A tax obligation needs a clear statutory basis. Many systems
  also read exemptions narrowly; others read the whole taxing provision
  neutrally. Anti-abuse rules let substance prevail over form; check whether
  the jurisdiction has a general one.
- **Fundamental rights.** Read rights broadly and their limitations
  narrowly. A limitation needs a legal basis, a legitimate aim, and
  proportionality.
- **Procedure.** Avoid excessive formalism: a procedural rule should not be
  read so as to deny access to a court when its purpose is otherwise met.
- **Private law.** Gaps are filled more readily, by analogy, general
  principles, and good faith. Contracts and wills are interpreted by the
  governing law's rules for private documents (common intent of the
  parties, the reasonable addressee, *contra proferentem* for standard
  terms), not by the statutory methods alone.
- **Consumer and employment law.** Protective purpose weighs heavily; in
  doubt, prefer the reading that protects the weaker party where the law
  says so.
- **Treaties and framework instruments.** See
  `families/international.md`. Broad, goal-oriented instruments (founding
  treaties, constitutions) give purpose and system more weight because
  their text is deliberately open.
