# Structuring the answer

Interpretation is justification: the reader must be able to follow and
challenge each step.

## Top level: question, rule, application, conclusion

1. **Question.** The precise interpretive point, as narrow as possible.
2. **Rule.** The provision (with its applicable version) and the arguments
   that bear on it, including the case law retrieved.
3. **Application.** Each method in turn: what reading it supports and how
   strongly. Note where the methods converge and where they conflict.
4. **Conclusion.** The reading adopted, the priority rule that settles any
   conflict, and the degree of confidence. Lead the answer with this.

## One contested point: claim, grounds, warrant, rebuttal

When a single argument carries the result, lay it out fully:

- **Claim:** the reading proposed.
- **Grounds:** the text, record, decisions, and facts relied on.
- **Warrant:** the interpretive argument linking grounds to claim
  (ordinary meaning, *lex specialis*, the purpose of the norm).
- **Backing:** why that argument has weight here (a decision applying it,
  the priority rules of the jurisdiction).
- **Qualifier:** how certain ("clearly", "on balance", "arguably").
- **Rebuttal:** the strongest counter-reading and the conditions under
  which it would win. Never omit it.

## Rights in tension: proportionality

When a reading limits a fundamental right or chooses between competing
principles, test it:

1. **Legitimate aim:** does the reading serve an aim the order recognises?
2. **Suitability:** does it advance that aim?
3. **Necessity:** is there a reading that serves the aim with less
   interference?
4. **Balance:** does the gain outweigh the harm to the competing right?

Jurisdictions phrase these steps differently; the overlay says how.

## Confidence

Grade the conclusion:

- **Settled:** apex case law on point, methods converge.
- **Strong:** methods converge, no case law on point, or case law on a
  closely analogous provision.
- **Contested:** methods or courts diverge; state both readings and which
  you prefer.
- **Open:** the text, the methods, and the courts leave it undetermined;
  say what would decide it.
